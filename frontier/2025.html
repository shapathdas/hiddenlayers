DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning

DeepSeek-AI

DeepSeek-R1 demonstrates that complex reasoning (reflection/exploration) can emerge through pure Reinforcement Learning without massive supervised datasets. It utilizes Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a high-efficiency algorithm that eliminates the need for a separate critic model by calculating advantages via relative group performance, significantly reducing training costs. This architecture has set a new benchmark for scalable reasoning models, inspiring widespread replication across specialized domains like math and vision.

On the Biology of a Large Language Model

Lindsey, Gurney et. al.

This paper advances mechanistic interpretability by moving beyond "black-box" analysis to map the internal computational circuits of Claude 3.5 Haiku. By employing attribution graphs and circuit-tracing, the researchers provide causal insights into how specific neural representations drive reasoning behaviors. This framework—drawing from neuroscience and computational biology—offers a rigorous method for validating model safety, ensuring transparency, and meeting emerging regulatory compliance standards for enterprise AI.

Titans: Learning to Memorize at Test Time

Ali Behrouz, Peilin Zhong, Vahab Mirrokni

This work proposes a unique approach of thinking about memory as an explicit test-time learner of associations between keys and values. It offers an elegant general solution to the majority of sequence tasks by delegating some computation and training to the test time, making a model to learn more general patterns during training. This NeurIPS paper is thought of as a worthy successor of the transformer architecture. The results are very promising.

VGGT: Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer

Jianyuan Wang, Minghao Chen, Nikita Karaev, Andrea Vedaldi, Christian Rupprecht, David Novotny

Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) is a feed-forward neural network that can directly estimate all key 3D scene properties for hundreds of input views. It achieves state-of-the-art results in multiple 3D tasks, including camera parameter estimation, Multiview depth estimation, dense point cloud reconstruction, and 3D point tracking. This is a strong 3D vision foundational model contribution.

Why Diffusion Models Don't Memorize: Implicit Dynamical Regularization

Tony Bonnaire, Raphaël Urfin, Giulio Biroli, Marc Mézard

Diffusion models have achieved remarkable success across a wide range of generative tasks. A key challenge is understanding the mechanisms that prevent their memorization of training data and allow generalization. In this work, the authors investigate the role of the training dynamics in the transition from generalization to memorization, explaining why Early stopping has a strong theoretically basis.

π0.5 : a Vision-Language-Action Model with Open-World Generalization

Kevin Black, Noah Brown, James Darpinian, Karan Dhabalia, Danny Driess, Adnan Esmail, Michael Equi, Chelsea Finn, Niccolo Fusai, Manuel Y. Galliker, Dibya Ghosh, Lachy Groom, Karol Hausman, Brian Ichter, Szymon Jakubczak, Tim Jones, Liyiming Ke, Devin LeBlanc, Sergey Levine, Adrian Li-Bell, Mohith Mothukuri, Suraj Nair, Karl Pertsch, Allen Z. Ren, Lucy Xiaoyang Shi, Laura Smith, Jost Tobias Springenberg, Kyle Stachowicz, James Tanner, Quan Vuong, Homer Walke, Anna Walling, Haohuan Wang, Lili Yu, Ury Zhilinsky

The paper provides strong empirical evidence that heterogeneous co-training, mixing real robot demonstrations with complementary multimodal supervision (e.g., semantic subtask prediction, detections, and web-derived vision-language signals), can produce a VLA policy that generalizes in open-world settings rather than only in curated lab scenarios. The central scientific contribution isn't a flashy new module, but a training/data thesis: by unifying "what the task means" (language + semantics) with "how to execute" (low-level action chunks) inside one model, the approach bridges the semantics–control gap that has historically limited end-to-end VLAs.

ARC-AGI-2: A New Challenge for Frontier AI Reasoning Systems

Francois Chollet, Mike Knoop, Gregory Kamradt, Bryan Landers, Henry Pinkard

ARC-AGI-2 is a genuinely valuable benchmark refresh because it fixes the two things that quietly make many "reasoning" leaderboards scientifically messy: comparability of splits and leakage/implicit tuning over time. The authors don't just assert "humans can do it", they run controlled human testing at scale (hundreds of participants, thousands of attempts) and then calibrate the public / semi-private / private subsets to essentially the same human difficulty (≤1pp mean difference), which makes progress interpretable rather than a split-selection artifact. On the model side, the initial reported baseline performance is extremely low (near-noise percentages on the semi-private set at release time), supporting the core claim that this benchmark sits in a real "human-easy / AI-hard" regime and exposes a meaningful frontier gap.

Simulating 500 million years of evolution with a language model

Thomas Hayes, Roshan Rao, Halil Akin, Nicholas J. Sofroniew, Deniz Oktay, Zeming Lin, Robert Verkuil, Vincent Q. Tran, Jonathan Deaton, Marius Wiggert, Rohil Badkundri, Irhum Shafkat, Jun Gong, Alexander Derry, Raul S. Molina, Neil Thomas, Yousuf A. Khan, Chetan Mishra, Carolyn Kim, Liam J. Bartie, Matthew Nemeth, Patrick D. Hsu, Tom Sercu, Salvatore Candido, Alexander Rives

This paper shows that language-model training can act as a scalable simulator of biological evolution, capturing long-term constraints and adaptations over hundreds of millions of years—something infeasible with classical evolutionary simulations.

A DNA language model based on multispecies alignment predicts the effects of genome-wide variants

Gonzalo Benegas, Carlos Albors, Alan J. Aw, Chengzhong Ye, Yun S. Song

It's important because it shows that aligning genomes across many species (MSA) can be used like "context" for a DNA language model, letting the model predict variant effects genome-wide much better, directly supporting prioritizing disease variants and interpreting noncoding DNA at scale.

Gated Attention for Large Language Models

Z. Qiu, Z. Wang, B. Zheng, et al.

The paper shows how a simple change to the sigmoid activation function (conceived via systematic empirical evaluation) can lead to improved training stability, long-context extrapolation, and add input-dependent sparsity into LLMs. A simple but impactful paper.

"Train for the Worst, Plan for the Best": Masked Diffusion

J. Kim, K. Shah, V. Kontonis, et al.

Masked diffusion models are upcoming and indeed offer significant advantages over autoregressive models. This paper introduces adaptive strategies to select the correct token generation order during inference, which helps further improve MDMs. This paper could have a strong impact on discrete generative models.

Artificial Hivemind: Homogenization of LM Outputs

L. Jiang, Y. Chai, M. Li, et al.

The paper creates an interesting and important data set to be able to characterise better the open-endedness or creativity of human capacities such as metaphor use. Careful analysis of AI performance on such data sets can help to better understand their successes and limitations.

Beyond "Aha!": Systematic Meta-Abilities Alignment

Z. Hu, Y. Wang, H. Dong, et al.

The paper takes an interesting approach to explicitly aligning and assessing LRMs for basic building blocks of logical inference (deduction, induction, abduction). Understanding AI 'reasoning' in terms of these foundational capacities will probably be an important goal in assessing their utility/reliability for a variety of real-world tasks and scenarios.
